The 45- and 180-Day 1031 Deadlines

Confirm the Exchange Question Before Reshaping the Sale

For The 45- and 180-Day 1031 Deadlines, the owner should ask a qualified intermediary, attorney, and tax adviser whether the relinquished interest, replacement property, taxpayer, vesting, timing, related parties, debt, and proposed structure fit the owner's situation. A mineral buyer can supply accurate transaction facts and dates, but should not promise exchange qualification or choose the owner's tax elections. That advice boundary should remain visible in emails, calendars, draft agreements, and settlement instructions from the first review forward. The file should also record who is responsible for each decision, when that decision is due, and which document carries it into closing.

Keep the Royalty Underwriting File Intact

A possible exchange does not change the need to identify the counties, tracts, interests, wells, depths, formations, fractions, effective date, title standard, adjustment mechanics, and retained rights in The 45- and 180-Day 1031 Deadlines. The buyer's production and development assumptions should remain documented even when closing timing or proceeds routing receives extra attention.

Coordinate the Intermediary Before Funds Move

The working file for The 45- and 180-Day 1031 Deadlines should identify the current owner, proposed seller, purchasing entity, qualified intermediary, escrow or closing agent, legal and tax advisers, assignment language, notice requirements, consideration estimate, expected adjustments, and intended proceeds path. Those parties and documents should be confirmed before the mineral sale closes or funds are released.

Run One Calendar for the Sale and One for the Exchange

The 45- and 180-Day 1031 Deadlines can involve offer expiration, purchase-agreement signing, title review, curative work, adjustment deadlines, deed execution, mineral closing, intermediary assignment, the 45-day identification period, and the 180-day acquisition period. Putting every event on one page helps the owner see where buyer diligence, title work, replacement-property decisions, and adviser approvals can collide.

Maintain a Non-Exchange Closing Path

The owner considering The 45- and 180-Day 1031 Deadlines should understand what happens if the exchange proceeds, changes, or does not occur. The purchase agreement, deed, title schedule, settlement statement, revenue allocation, and buyer funding conditions still need to work as a mineral sale. A backup path should not be interpreted as tax advice, but it prevents unclear exchange assumptions from making the underlying conveyance vague.
